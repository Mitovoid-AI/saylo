How it works

Three moves. No new habits.

Saylo sits quietly in your tray. It only listens while you hold the keys.

Ctrl + Win
01

Hold

Click into any text box, then hold the shortcut.

02

Speak

Ramble, pause, change your mind halfway. Talk like you would to a friend.

Aa
03

Let go

Clean, punctuated text appears where you were typing.

Features

It writes what you meant.

Not a word-for-word transcript. Saylo edits as you speak, without adding anything you didn't say.

Drops the filler

Ums, uhs, “like”, “you know” and repeated words are gone.

So um I think I think we're ready.
→ I think we're ready.

Follows your corrections

Change your mind mid-sentence and Saylo keeps only the final version.

Meet at five, no wait, six.
→ Meet at six.

Makes lists

Say “first… second… third…” and get a numbered list.

→ 1. Open the app.
2. Click save.

Any app

Email, chat, docs, code editors, browser forms. If it has a cursor, Saylo can type there.

About a second

Typical time from letting go to text on screen is under a second and a half.

Your history stays home

Past dictations are saved only on your PC, and you can turn that off. Saylo has no server of its own.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Saylo free?

Yes. Saylo uses Groq's speech and language models, and you plug in your own Groq API key, which is free for personal use within Groq's limits. A paid version that needs no key is coming in the next update.

How do I get a Groq key?

Sign in at console.groq.com/keys, click “Create API Key” and paste it into Saylo during setup. It takes about a minute. The key is stored only on your computer.

What happens to my voice?

Saylo records only while you hold the shortcut. The clip goes directly from your PC to Groq to be turned into text and cleaned up; Saylo has no server in between. Your history is kept on your PC only, and you can switch it off or delete it.

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”. Is that safe?

That warning appears for new apps that aren't code-signed yet. Click “More info”, then “Run anyway”. The installer is built automatically from the public code in the GitHub repository.

Which languages does it support?

English for now.

Can I change the shortcut?

Not yet. On Windows it's Ctrl + Win, and in Chrome it's Alt + Shift, held down while you speak.

Why isn't the Chrome extension in the Web Store yet?

The listing is on its way. Until then, install it with “Load unpacked” as shown above; Chrome shows a small developer-mode notice for extensions installed this way.
